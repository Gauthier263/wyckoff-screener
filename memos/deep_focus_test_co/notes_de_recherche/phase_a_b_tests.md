## Q1. How do the main sources define the Secondary Test (Phase A) and its criteria? Volume and spread, location relative to the SC and AR, how close it must come, and what happens if the ST undercuts the SC

### Takeaway
All the near-primary sources agree on four points:
- **What it is.** The ST revisits *the area* of the SC to test the supply/demand balance there.
- **Signature.** Volume and spread are "significantly diminished" relative to the SC: "less selling than previously".
- **Where it stops.** It "generally" stops at or above the SC low.
- **What it sets.** It closes Phase A and, with the SC low and the AR high, sets the range boundaries.

No source gives a numeric distance to the SC. The sources split on what an undercut means:
- **StockCharts/Wyckoff Analytics (Bogomazov–Lipsett):** an ST below the SC means "either new lows or prolonged consolidation".
- **Villahermosa:** allows a slight undercut and reads it as seller imbalance.
- **Fraser:** if the decline after the SC makes a new low, the SC→ST segment may not be accumulation at all, so the P&F count should start from the ST.

#### Definitions of the ST (accumulation, Phase A)
- StockCharts/WA, glossary: "ST—secondary test, in which price revisits the area of the SC to test the supply/demand balance at these levels. If a bottom is to be confirmed, volume and price spread should be significantly diminished as the market approaches support in the area of the SC. It is common to have multiple STs after a SC." — [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- StockCharts/WA, Phase A text:
  - "A successful secondary test (ST) in the area of the SC will show less selling than previously and a narrowing of spread and decreased volume, generally stopping at or above the same price level as the SC. If the ST goes lower than that of the SC, one can anticipate either new lows or prolonged consolidation. The lows of the SC and the ST and the high of the AR set the boundaries of the TR."
  - Source: [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- StockCharts/WA, generic "Test":
  - "Large operators always test the market for supply throughout a TR (e.g., STs and springs) and at key points during a price advance. If considerable supply emerges on a test, the market is often not ready to be marked up."
  - Also: "a successful test … typically makes a higher low on lesser volume."
  - Source: [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- Pruden & von Lichtenstein 2006:
  - Definition: "(STs) Secondary Test(s)… revisit the area of the Selling Climax to test the supply demand balance at these price levels. If a bottom is to be confirmed, significant supply should not resurface, and volume and price spread should be significantly diminished as the market approaches support in the area of the SC."
  - Phase A text: "A Secondary Test on the downside usually involves less selling than on the SC and with a narrowing of spread and decreased volume. The lows of the Selling Climax (SC) and the Secondary Test, and the high of the Automatic Rally (AR) initially set the boundaries of the trading range."
  - Source: [Pruden & von Lichtenstein 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)
- Fraser: "Phase A is fulfilled with a test of the SCLX and is labeled ST." — [Fraser, Let's Review (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-07-wyckoff-power-charting-lets-review)
- Fraser, case study: "A big bulge of volume on a SCLX will be tested at a later date, which occurs here at the ST." Also: "When a low is made on very high volume, expect a price retest at a later date … An LPS or a Spring are doing the same type of work; which is to test a prior high volume price low." — [Fraser, Phase Analysis. Two Case Studies (Aug 2016)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2016-08-phase-analysis-two-case-studies/)
- Weis states the same principle as a general rule: "Watch for tests of high‐volume or 'vertical' areas where price accelerated upward or downward." — [Weis, Introduction (Wiley sample)](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-802008-2fe9f872b5.pdf)
- Villahermosa (tradingwyckoff.com), function of the ST:
  - "the confirmation of the abandonment by aggressive sellers who have been pushing the price ever lower … to evolve toward an equilibrium environment where buyers and sellers are comfortable negotiating (building the cause for the subsequent effect)."
  - Criteria: "For the Secondary Test to be successful, the bearish movement must be made with a narrowing in price ranges and lower volume than seen in the Selling Climax."
  - Priority: "What we really need to focus on is, in order of importance: the decrease in volume and the narrowing of ranges."
  - Source: [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)

### Inferences
- **The CO's question at the Phase-A ST:** "Is there still significant supply at the price where the public panicked (SC)?"
  - Diminished volume and spread on the return = sellers have largely been absorbed. The TR is defined (SC low, ST low, AR high) and the CO can begin Phase B absorption.
  - Heavy volume, a wide spread and a close near the low on the return = "considerable supply" is still there. The market is "not ready" (tutorial). Expect either new lows (the stopping action failed) or a longer range in which the CO keeps absorbing.
- **Reference volume.** Doctrine measures the ST against the **SC** (and later against previous tests), not against a moving average.
  - With the screener's climax ≥ 2.0×MA20 and test_vol ≤ 0.85×MA20, an ST that passes has at most ~42% of the SC's volume when the SC sits exactly at 2.0×. This is in the same ballpark as the website FAQ's "40–60% of SC", which is itself a heuristic.
  - Recommendation: compute and display the ratio **ST_vol / SC_vol** and **ST_spread / SC_spread** in addition to the MA/ATR-relative values.
  - Caveat: ATR right after a climax is inflated by the SC bars, so "≤ 0.7 ATR" is easier to meet in the bars just after the SC.
- **Location grading for the screener**, consistent with the sources:
  - ST low ≥ SC low → "ST (Phase A) — neutral/valid".
  - ST ends in the upper half of the SC→AR range → "buyer imbalance" (Villahermosa).
  - Slight undercut of the SC → "ST below SC — seller imbalance: expect new lows or a long consolidation" (tutorial). Do **not** label it "SPRING" this early: a spring presupposes a support "established in Phases A and B" (tutorial, Phase C text).
- **When there is no climax** (common in re-accumulation, or a non-climactic Phase A), the Phase-A "ST" label may not apply at all. This is consistent with the CLAUDE.md top-down rule: in a downtrend, a climactic stop may start a *redistribution* (tutorial, redistribution note).

## Q2. What exactly are "ST in Phase B", "ST as SOW" and "UA" in the StockCharts / Wyckoff Analytics schematics versus other sources, and what does each signal for the CO's campaign?

### Takeaway
The published StockCharts / Wyckoff Analytics schematics (Bogomazov, edited by Pruden) label only **"ST in Phase B"**. It is a Phase-B test of the lows drawn **below the SC low**. They do **not** contain "UA" or "ST as SOW".
- The upper-boundary test in accumulation Phase B is drawn (a peak above the AR, creating a second resistance line) but left unlabeled. The text calls it "upthrust-type actions".
- "UA" (Upthrust Action) and "ST as SOW" are Villahermosa's labels, also seen in TradingView education material. In his scheme the same price action is labeled differently depending on the presumed bias:
  - upper-end test: UA in accumulation vs UT in distribution;
  - lower-low test: ST as SOW in accumulation vs mSOW in distribution;
  - "ST in B" as the neutral label until the range resolves.
- Fraser uses "UT" for accumulation Phase-B upthrusts.

#### Villahermosa's definitions (tradingwyckoff.com)
- Phase B composition: "Phase B is composed of successive tests (Secondary Test in B) that can occur at both the upper and lower extremes of the structure". He lists "UA / UT — Upthrust Action or Upthrust" and "Secondary Test as Sign of Weakness or minor Sign Of Weakness". — [Villahermosa, Phases](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/phases-of-a-wyckoff-structure/)
- Status of the ST label: "Although the 'official' Secondary Test is the one that appears in Phase A, it is a type of behavior that we will continue to observe in different phases of the structure's development." Also: "This type of test serves to evaluate the strength and weakness of buyers and sellers." — [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)
- **Upper end (UA vs UT):**
  - "The price crosses the previous high created in the stopping action but does not move too far before re-entering within the range, leaving a slight shakeout."
  - "When the range is accumulation/reaccumulation, this event will be labeled as Upthrust Action (UA); while if it is a distribution or redistribution structure we will label it as Upthrust (UT). This is the only difference between these labels".
  - "A subsequent evaluation will confirm whether it is really a strength test in which stock has been absorbed … or whether it is an action on which it has been distributed".
  - Source: [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)
- **mSOS variant:** "When the UA occurs and the price stays above resistance for some time before falling, this behavior can be labeled as minor Sign Of Strength (mSOS). It would be a type of test that denotes greater strength." — [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)
- **Lower end (ST as SOW vs mSOW):**
  - "It is a test to the lows of the structure that produces a lower low. It is due to either the aggressiveness of sellers or a lack of interest from buyers; which suggests that new tests to that area are likely in the future."
  - "From this new low we can draw another support level".
  - In accumulation: "Secondary Test as Sign of Weakness (ST as SOW). Generally there are more chances of this event appearing when the Secondary Test of Phase A has produced a lower low."
  - In distribution/redistribution: "minor Sign Of Weakness (mSOW). An indication that we may be facing a mSOW is if the Secondary Test of Phase A is a poor bullish movement, with very little travel (lack of buyer interest)."
  - Source: [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)
- **Neutral labelling:** "until the price leaves the range we cannot know what the real intention was behind the cause being built and therefore, in real time any labeling should be valid". Also: "a simple solution could be to label such events as Secondary Test in Phase B (ST in B), a label you will also find in some methodology analyses." — [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)
- **What each signals for the next phase:**
  - "If we have previously identified a Secondary Test in the form of Upthrust Action (UA), this denotes greater strength to buyers and therefore in the zone of structure lows breakout we favor the Spring instead of the bearish breakout."
  - "If what price has left is a Secondary Test in the form of Sign of Weakness (ST as SOW), it denotes seller strength and in the zone of structure highs breakout we favor the Upthrust instead of the effective bullish breakout."
  - Spring checklist: "Prior Secondary Test with strength signal: Ideally Upthrust Action (UA) in Phase B".
  - Source: [Villahermosa, Spring/Shakeout](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/spring-shakeout/)
- **Website FAQ (heuristic, flagged):** "Multiple Secondary Tests at support (SC area) with low volume validate support. Upthrust Actions (UA) at resistance (AR area) with moderate volume that fail validate resistance." Also: "Each subsequent test … should show lower volume than the previous one". — [Villahermosa site, Phases FAQ](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/phases-of-a-wyckoff-structure/)

### Inferences
**Campaign meaning of each label (synthesis):**

| Label | The CO's question | What a "good" answer looks like | What the CO does next |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ST in Phase B** (lows) | "How much floating supply is still in weak hands at the lows, and is it shrinking?" | Successive pushes to support on decreasing volume/spread | Keep absorbing; once supply looks exhausted, move to the Phase C test |
| **UA** (upper end, accumulation bias) | "How much supply sits above the AR, and how strong is demand when price pokes through?" | Quick, shallow re-entry with little supply response | Mirrors the tutorial's "checking advances in price with short sales": the CO caps premature advances. Per Villahermosa, it tilts expectations toward a later Spring rather than a breakdown |
| **ST as SOW** (lower low in Phase B, accumulation bias) | "Is supply still aggressive / are buyers absent?" | n/a: a lower low means supply is still in control locally | That zone must be retested; Phase B is likely to extend. Villahermosa: the eventual test of the highs is more likely to be an upthrust than a real breakout. Under the CLAUDE.md top-down rule, this keeps the redistribution scenario alive |

**Screener labelling consequences:**
- Use **conditional labels** in Phase B ("ST-B (low test)", "UA/UT? (upper test)", "ST-B lower low (ST as SOW / mSOW?)"). Fix them only after the range resolves (Villahermosa; CLAUDE.md conditional labelling).
- **Upper-end test:** the label should record whether price **re-entered quickly** (UA/UT) or **held above resistance for some time** (mSOS candidate).
- **Lower low in Phase B:** this should not trigger "SPRING" if Phase B is young. Villahermosa: a spring comes "after completing Phase B (after several Secondary Tests)". The schematic itself shows a Phase-B lower low before the Phase-C spring.
- **Volume on Phase-B STs:** early ones can carry high volume (Fraser: "typically have high volume"). A strict test_vol ≤ 0.85× filter would miss them. Better to label them as tests with supply still present and track whether volume declines across successive tests.

## Q3. What does the CO do during Phase B, how do successive tests and volume reveal campaign maturity, and how does Phase B duration relate to the cause (P&F count)?

### Takeaway
Phase B is where the cause is built. The CO buys on balance (net accumulation) while selling or shorting into rallies to cap price, and lets volatility shake out weak holders. Both boundaries get tested, sometimes with overshoots on either side.

Maturity shows in four places:
- **Swings:** wide, high-volume swings early on that narrow and quiet down.
- **Downswing volume:** volume on downswings to support shrinks.
- **Effort vs result:** rallies get easier relative to reactions; volume rises on rallies and falls on reactions (buying test #3).
- **Failures:** failures at the highs or lows get progressively less convincing.

Phase B lasts as long as supply (or demand, in distribution) remains to be absorbed. A longer, more complete Phase B means a bigger P&F horizontal count, i.e., a bigger cause. Only the part that is genuinely accumulation (or distribution) should be counted.

### Inferences
**Maturity scorecard for Phase B (accumulation; mirror it for distribution):**
1. Volume on each successive push to support is lower than on the previous one, and lower than on the SC.
2. Spreads on those pushes narrow; closes come off the lows (CLV rising).
3. Rallies travel as far or farther on equal or lower volume (effort vs result). Reactions get shorter in time and extent (Fraser's "duration and extent").
4. Overshoots at the lows (ST in B lower low) **stop producing follow-through** and get retested on lighter volume.
5. Volume rises on rallies and falls on reactions (buying test #3).
6. Overall swing amplitude contracts.

Early in Phase B, high-volume tests are *expected* (tutorial, Pruden, Fraser). The signal is the **trend across tests**, not one bar.

**Screener implementation ideas** (internal heuristics, to be validated in backtest):
- Store the sequence of Phase-B tests with vol×MA, vol/SC_vol, spread/ATR and CLV.
- Flag "absorption progressing" when the last 2–3 low tests show decreasing vol/SC_vol and spread, and none of them extends materially below the prior test low.
- Flag "supply still present / ST as SOW" when a test makes a lower low on rising volume and a wide spread with a low close.
- The fixed test_vol ≤ 0.85×MA20 rule is a reasonable *late-Phase-B / Phase-C* test filter. It should not gate the *recognition* of early Phase-B STs.

**Time proportion as a guard against premature "Spring" labels.**
- Example rule: bars since SC (Phase B length) ≥ Phase A length (SC→ST bars). This follows Villahermosa's "longer than Phases A and C"; the exact rule is an internal design choice.
- On H1/H4 crypto, Phase B lengths are in bars. No source gives bar counts.

**Cause / P&F.**
- If the SC→first-ST leg contains a new low with a big decline (Fraser's ALB pattern), count the cause only from the ST (segment A). Add segment B only after the outcome confirms it. This avoids over-optimistic targets.
- A longer, cleaner Phase B means a bigger count. A short Phase B means either urgency (stronger move, per Villahermosa) or an incomplete cause.

**Fractal link (CLAUDE.md):** Villahermosa's practical advice when trading Phase B extremes is to drop to a lower timeframe. "if we are in the upper zone, we will look for a minor distribution structure; and if we are in the lower zone, we will look for a minor accumulation structure" ([Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)). So an H4 "ST in B" can be a complete H1 accumulation, and an H4 UA/UT can be a complete H1 distribution. This matches the screener's fractal convention.

## Q4. The distribution mirror: PSY, BC, AR, ST of the BC (lower high? lower volume?), UT in Phase B vs a genuine SOS, and the "SOW in Phase B" / minor SOW. Signatures and meaning

### Takeaway
In distribution, the ST revisits the BC area and should show the demand side failing. Most sources expect diminished volume and spread on the approach to the BC:
- the tutorial/WA text;
- Pruden 2006;
- the S&C 2014 legend.

Fraser instead describes the ST peak as marked by "high volume and reversal price bars", read as the CO selling. This is a genuine difference in emphasis.

On the shape of the ST and the UT:
- The idealised ST is a slightly **lower high**.
- The ST "may take the form of an upthrust (UT)" above the BC that quickly closes back below resistance.
- In Phase B, the UT is a "brief", "minor breakout" that "will not linger". By contrast, a genuine SOS (in accumulation) breaks out on wide spread and higher volume, **holds**, and is followed by a backup on lighter volume.
- The "SOW in Phase B" (StockCharts schematic) / "mSOW" (Villahermosa) is a Phase-B drop that undercuts the AR low on expanding volume and spread. It is an early clue that supply is gaining.

  - "A jump is a relatively wider price-spread move made on comparatively higher volume that penetrates outer resistance or support. A backup is a test that immediately follows the jump – a relatively narrow price-spread reaction or rally on comparatively lighter volume that tests and confirms the legitimacy of the preceding jump action."
  - "according to Wyckoff, you should not buy breakouts because that would leave you vulnerable to swift moves in the opposite direction if the breakout turned out to be false."
  - Source: [Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)
- Fraser (MU, TWTR):
  - On a quality SOS: "the stock price has the power to jump above the prior UT highs and the price holds above the Accumulation area."
  - "After a rally to $21 we watch for a SOS that can hold above Resistance. The less price retraces back into the Accumulation the better."
  - "The Backups should have the signature of shrinking volume and spread to prove absorption has taken place."
  - Source: [Fraser, Phase Analysis. Two Case Studies (2016)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2016-08-phase-analysis-two-case-studies/)
- Villahermosa: a UA that "stays above resistance for some time before falling" = "minor Sign Of Strength (mSOS)". This is an intermediate case between a failed poke and a real SOS. — [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)

#### SOW in Phase B / minor SOW
- StockCharts/WA: "SOW—sign of weakness, observable as a down-move to (or slightly past) the lower boundary of the TR, usually occurring on increased spread and volume. The AR and the initial SOW(s) indicate a change of character in the price action of the stock: supply is now dominant." Also, from the distribution Phase B text: "SOWs are usually accompanied by significantly increased spread and volume to the downside." — [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- Fraser: "After a ST or UT, the drop to Support is marked by wide declining price bars and expanding volume. At the conclusion of the decline, price breaches the Support line (drawn at the AR low) and the prior low(s) set by the ST decline. Often the SOW indicates a subtle change of character". — [Fraser, Distribution Definitions (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-distribution-definitions/)
- Fraser (CVX, KLAC):
  - "The SOW undercutting the AR is another subtle clue that Distribution is at hand."
  - "In Phase B the UT is followed by a rapid decline into a SOW. This puts us on alert that the Distribution is progressing in a hurry."
  - Source: [Fraser, Just Another Phase (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-just-another-phase/)
- Villahermosa: in distribution/redistribution the Phase-B lower low is labelled "mSOW". One clue is "if the Secondary Test of Phase A is a poor bullish movement, with very little travel (lack of buyer interest)". — [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)
- Distribution Phase B purpose: "institutions and large professional interests are disposing of their long inventory and initiating short positions … the large interests are net sellers of shares as the TR evolves, with the goal of exhausting as much of the remaining demand as possible." — [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- Fraser: "The B phase is typically a long trendless trading range where stock is being sold in large quantities by the C.O. Continued retesting of the high price area brings comfort to stockholders, at their peril." — [Fraser, Just Another Phase (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-just-another-phase/)

### Inferences
**The CO's questions in distribution:**
- At the **ST of the BC:** "Is there still enough demand at the top to absorb my supply?"
  - A lower high on drying volume/spread = demand fading (tutorial / Pruden / S&C reading).
  - A high-volume reversal bar at the ST peak = the CO's supply hitting the bid (Fraser reading).
  - Both point the same way **if the close is weak**. The volume difference is about *where* you measure: the approach rally (diminishing) vs the reversal bars (heavy).
- At a **UT in Phase B:** "Can I lure breakout demand and sell into it?" Fraser: momentum traders are "a source of demand the C.O. can sell shares into".
- At the **SOW in Phase B / mSOW:** "How much demand is left under the AR?" An undercut on expanding volume/spread with weak closes = supply gaining.

**UT (Phase B) vs genuine SOS: verification checklist** (combines Pruden's jump/backup definition, Fraser's "will not linger" and "holds above", and the tutorial's SOS definition):
- **(a) Acceptance:** does price close and stay above resistance for several bars (SOS / mSOS candidate), or re-enter quickly (UT/UA)?
- **(b) Close location on the breakout bars:** strong closes (high CLV) vs closes back below the line.
- **(c) Volume on the pullback:** a backup on narrower spread and lighter volume = confirmation (Pruden). Heavy volume and a wide spread back into the range = UT.
- **(d) Phase position:** before any Phase C test, a breakout is presumptively a UT/UA. This is the CLAUDE.md rule: "un breakout n'est un SOS qu'après tenue + back-up (LPS)".
- **(e) Prior Phase-B evidence:** a prior mSOW or Phase-B SOW raises the odds that the poke is a UT. A prior UA or mSOS raises the odds of a real SOS.

**Screener mapping:**
- A UT bar can easily satisfy SOS ≥ 1.3×vol and wide ≥ 1.3 ATR. So these thresholds alone cannot separate UT from SOS.
- Add the **acceptance test**: N bars closing beyond the level.
- Add the **backup test**: next pullback vol ≤ test_vol and spread ≤ narrow, holding above the broken level.
- Add the **reclaim/rejection test**: the CLV of the bar that re-enters the range. CLV ≤ 0.5 on the re-entry bar marks a UT. This is the same CLV logic as the reclaim rule, mirrored.

**OI overlay for perps** (inference only; no source covers OI). Under the CLAUDE.md conventions:
- A UT that breaks out with OI coin rising and then re-enters = trapped new longs.
- A real SOS should hold with OI rising and a lower-volume backup.
- Cross-check with funding, liquidations and the L/S ratio when price stalls, as the project rules require.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how far a UT may exceed the BC, or how many bars defines "will not linger". Both are qualitative.
- The Fraser vs tutorial/Pruden/S&C difference on ST-of-BC volume was not reconciled by any author explicitly. The reconciliation above (approach vs reversal bars) is my inference.
- No source discusses whether the Phase-A ST of a BC typically shows rising Open Interest (crypto-specific).

## Q5. Common labelling mistakes, disagreements between sources, and practitioner checklists for Phases A–B (with mapping to the screener's thresholds)

### Takeaway
The recurring mistakes the sources warn about:
- treating the first climax/range as proven accumulation;
- labelling a Phase-B overshoot as a Spring or SOS (UT) too early;
- fading or chasing range edges against the context;
- over-counting the P&F cause from an SC that was not yet real absorption;
- refusing to relabel when price does the unexpected.

The sources disagree on:
- ST volume in distribution (diminished vs high at the reversal);
- whether an ST may undercut the SC;
- naming (UA vs UT in accumulation; "ST in Phase B" vs "ST as SOW"; "SOW in Phase B" vs "mSOW");
- whether Phase A must be climactic;
- the very legitimacy of the event templates (Weis: created by associates, not Wyckoff).

### Cited Findings

#### Mistakes and warnings stated by sources
- **Presuming accumulation from a climax:**
  - Pruden: "it may be accumulation, redistribution or nothing." — [Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)
  - Fraser after a BC/AR: "we are now on the lookout for either a Stepping Stone Reaccumulation or a Distribution formation." — [Fraser, Distribution Definitions (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-distribution-definitions/)
  - Tutorial on redistribution's accumulation-like Phase A. — [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- **Over-counting the cause:** "Over-counting a PnF Accumulation range is a common problem. Segmenting the PnF count is the answer to this potentially costly error." — [Fraser, Segmenting PnF Counts (2017)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts/)
- **Fading the range top out of context:**
  - "traders often get in trouble at the end of Accumulation because they continually sell short the Resistance Line area."
  - "Accumulation Schematic #2 sets up a false short sale scenario for speculators after successful short sale campaigns in Phase B."
  - Source: [Fraser, Let's Review (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-07-wyckoff-power-charting-lets-review)
- **Shorting UTs too early:** "the 'smart money' repeatedly stops out traders who initiate such short positions with one UT after another". — [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- **Buying breakouts:** "you should not buy breakouts because that would leave you vulnerable to swift moves in the opposite direction if the breakout turned out to be false." — [Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)
- **Judgment calls on where Phase A ends:** "We mark the first minor Test as the completion of Phase A and the AR follows. This is a judgment call as the normal protocol is to look for a ST after the AR low and that is the conclusion of Phase A." — [Fraser, Just Another Phase (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-just-another-phase/)
- **Failing to relabel:** "we would relabel Phase C and D as a continuation of Phase B" and "We must continually be ready to update our scenarios and labeling". — [Fraser, Phase Analysis. Two Case Studies (2016)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2016-08-phase-analysis-two-case-studies/)
- **Rigid pattern-matching:** "These patterns can quickly become cookie prints, like geometric formations, into which price movement is stuffed by those looking for a quick, no‐think fix. They lead to rigid rather than creative thinking." — [Weis, Introduction (Wiley sample)](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-802008-2fe9f872b5.pdf)
- **Firm labels in real time:** "until the price leaves the range we cannot know what the real intention was … in real time any labeling should be valid". Hence the neutral "ST in B". — [Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)
- **Premature Spring:** "A 'premature' Spring, without cause built, fails much more often". — [Villahermosa, Spring/Shakeout](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/spring-shakeout/)
- **Variations:** "an incalculable number of variations can be revealed during the Distribution Phase. Mastery is seeing the overall principles (Phases) at work". — [Fraser, Distribution Definitions (2015)](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-distribution-definitions/)

#### Explicit disagreements / inconsistencies between sources
1. **Volume at the ST of the BC.**
   - Diminished: "volume and spread should thus decrease" ([StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)); "volume and spread should be diminished" ([Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)); "narrowing price spread and diminished volume" ([S&C 2014](https://www.wyckoffanalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/V32C08822PRUD.pdf)).
   - High: "The peak of the ST will generally be marked by high volume and reversal price bars" ([Fraser, Distribution Definitions](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2015-09-distribution-definitions/)).
2. **Volume on Phase-B STs in accumulation.**
   - The ST definition asks for "significantly diminished" volume ([StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)).
   - Fraser says Phase-B declines to support "typically have high volume (evidence of Supply)" ([Fraser, Segmenting PnF Counts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2017-05-segmenting-pnf-counts/)).
   - The tutorial itself concedes that early Phase-B swings come "accompanied by high volume" ([StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)).
3. **Can the ST undercut the SC?**
   - "generally stopping at or above"; an undercut means "new lows or prolonged consolidation" ([StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)).
   - The schematic shows the Phase-B ST below the SC ([Accumulation Schematic #1 image](https://436553459-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2FERtrZrZOhufFzk6ZQO4B%2Fuploads%2FlkRlzRrgfyYw8IaVt1Cy%2Fwyckoffaccumulation.png?alt=media&token=d702e0e7-9428-465d-8f93-8a6250305617)).
   - Villahermosa: "some authors defend the position that it is necessary for the Secondary Test to remain above the low … it would be convenient to grant some flexibility" ([Villahermosa, Secondary Test](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)).
4. **Naming.**
   - Accumulation Phase-B upper test: unlabeled "upthrust-type actions" ([StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)) vs "UT" ([Fraser 2016](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-wyckoff-2016-08-phase-analysis-two-case-studies/)) vs "UA" ([Villahermosa](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)).
   - Lower-low test: "ST in Phase B" (StockCharts schematic) vs "ST as SOW" (Villahermosa).
   - Distribution lower low: "SOW in Phase B" ([Distribution Schematic #1 image](https://436553459-files.gitbook.io/~/files/v0/b/gitbook-x-prod.appspot.com/o/spaces%2FERtrZrZOhufFzk6ZQO4B%2Fuploads%2FsvRDOoS9B6sZSwG1NtYs%2Fwyckoffdistribution1.png?alt=media&token=51e948b0-6ded-4c39-ab5f-f466654e166e)) vs "mSOW" ([Villahermosa](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/secondary-test/)).
5. **"Secondary test" is also used for the test after a Phase-C shakeout.** Pruden 2006: "The shakeout at point 8 … represents our first prescribed place to initiate a long position. The secondary test at point 10 is better, since a low volume pullback and a specific low risk stop". — [Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)
6. **Must Phase A be climactic?**
   - No: the tutorial and Pruden ("without dramatic changes in spread and volume") — [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial); [Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf).
   - Villahermosa's main article: "We should identify some type of climactic volume, whether in the preliminary stop or the climax" — [Villahermosa, Phases](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/phases-of-a-wyckoff-structure/).
   - His site FAQ: "4 mandatory sequential events" (flagged FAQ) — [Villahermosa site, Phases FAQ](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/phases-of-a-wyckoff-structure/).
7. **Role of volume in judging tests.**
   - Villahermosa (spring article, his own practice): "In my trading, volume is secondary information … what defines the quality of the shakeout is how price behaves after the breakout". — [Villahermosa, Spring/Shakeout](https://tradingwyckoff.com/en/spring-shakeout/)
   - The classic texts make volume the arbiter of tests: "A low-volume spring … indicates that the stock is likely to be ready to move up". — [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
8. **Provenance of the templates.**
   - Weis: the schematics were "created by his former associates", and Wyckoff "never used such terminology" (springs, upthrusts, ice) ([Weis, Introduction](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-802008-2fe9f872b5.pdf)).
   - Pruden: many annotations "reflect the work of Mr. Robert G. Evans" ([Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)).

#### Checklists present in the sources
- Wyckoff nine buying tests:
  - Test #2: "Preliminary support, selling climax, secondary test".
  - Test #3: "Activity bullish (volume increases on rallies and diminishes during reactions)".
  - Plus "Higher lows", "Higher highs", "Base forming", etc.
  - The selling tests mirror these: "Activity bearish (volume decreases on rallies and increases on reactions)", "Preliminary supply, buying climax".
  - Source: [StockCharts tutorial](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/market-analysis/wyckoff-analysis-articles/the-wyckoff-method-a-tutorial)
- Pruden's analytical checklist: "When analysing a TR, we are first seeking to uncover what the law of supply and demand is revealing to us. However, when individual movements, rallies or reactions are not revealing … it is important to remember the law of 'effort versus result'. By comparing rallies and reactions within the trading range to each other in terms of spread, volume, velocity and price, additional clues may be given". — [Pruden 2006](https://www.prorealcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/WyckoffSchematics-VisualTemplatesForMarketTimingDecisions.pdf)
- Weis's reading rules:
  - "Watch for follow‐through or lack of follow‐through after penetrations of support/resistance (this includes the notion of springs and upthrusts)."
  - "Watch for tests of high‐volume or 'vertical' areas where price accelerated upward or downward."
  - Source: [Weis, Introduction (Wiley sample)](https://content.e-bookshelf.de/media/reading/L-802008-2fe9f872b5.pdf)

### Inferences
**Consolidated Phase A–B practitioner checklist** (synthesised from the sources above; ordered per the CLAUDE.md hierarchy volume → OI → tertiary metrics):
1. **Context first.** Name the HTF trend and timeframe. A climactic stop inside a downtrend is *redistribution until proven otherwise* (CLAUDE.md; tutorial redistribution note; Pruden "accumulation, redistribution or nothing"). Hold both scenarios.
2. **Climax.** Is volume clearly climactic relative to the swing? Screener: ≥ 2.0×MA20, wide ≥ 1.3 ATR. For an SC, does the close come "well off the low" (CLV high)? If Phase A is non-climactic, expect re-accumulation or redistribution structures, and do not force PS/SC/ST labels.
3. **AR.** A sharp, brief reaction that sets the other boundary. Draw the lines at the SC low / ST low and the AR high (BC high and AR low in distribution).
4. **Phase-A ST.** Price returns to the area of the SC/BC on clearly lower volume and narrower spread than the climax.
   - Screener: test_vol ≤ 0.85×MA20 and narrow ≤ 0.7 ATR. Also compute vol/SC_vol and spread/SC_spread.
   - Location: at or above the SC low (below or at the BC high). An undercut means expecting new lows or a long range.
5. **Phase B: do not trade the middle.** Track every test of each edge:
   - volume, spread and close on each push;
   - follow-through or not after each penetration;
   - duration and extent of rallies vs reactions.
   - Early high volume is normal. A *declining* sequence is the absorption signal.
6. **Overshoots.**
   - Lower low in B: "ST in B / ST as SOW (acc) / mSOW (dist)". Redraw support and expect a retest; not a Spring yet.
   - Higher high in B: "UA (acc) / UT (dist)". Redraw resistance. Only acceptance plus a lighter-volume backup upgrades it to an mSOS/SOS candidate.
7. **OI then tertiary metrics** (crypto adaptation; no literature). On a test of the lows:
   - OI coin falling on low volume = positions closing, supply not re-engaging.
   - OI coin rising with volume and a lower low = new shorts pressing. This is consistent with "ST as SOW"; confirm with L/S ratio, funding and liquidations if price stalls.
8. **Time and cause.** Phase B should outlast Phase A before a Phase-C Spring/UTAD is credited (Villahermosa proportion). Segment the P&F count and do not count an SC→ST stretch that shows no absorption (Fraser).
9. **Relabel immediately** when price does the unexpected (Fraser; CLAUDE.md re-evaluation rule).

**Labelling discipline for the screener's output:**
- Keep "ST" for the Phase-A test only.
- Use "ST-B" for later edge tests with a direction suffix.
- Attach the conditional alternative ("UA/UT?", "ST-as-SOW/mSOW?") until the range resolves.
- Never auto-label "SPRING" or "SOS" for an event inside Phase B.

### Gaps
- No dedicated practitioner "common labelling mistakes" list from Wyckoff Analytics (Bogomazov) was found in public material. The mistakes above are assembled from scattered statements.
- No source provides numeric validation (backtested hit rates) for any Phase A–B test criterion. All criteria in the literature are qualitative. The screener's numeric thresholds remain internal heuristics to be validated with the existing `optimize.py` IS/OOS framework.
- Weis's detailed treatment of tests, springs/upthrusts and absorption (chapters 5–7) could not be accessed beyond the introduction.
